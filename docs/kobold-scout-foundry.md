# Kobold Scout — first skulk profile, 0.22.0

Version **0.22.0** adds an explicit **no-ammunition melee fallback** and a spatial tactical map. A missing or zero-quantity bolt stack makes drawing a shortsword worthwhile even outside adjacency and without a swarm opportunity. Melee approach uses route progress to the sword's actual reach and keeps hand/action costs. An empty-crossbow scout does not continue ranged kiting or collect sight rewards while unable to fire. Healing HP does not restore ammunition, loaded state or held equipment; these remain persistent resources.

### Tactical grid and movement memory

The bounded map uses native ground-movement constraints and terrain measurement to build grid connections. It estimates pursuer arrival for up to three movement actions, with footprints up to four squares per side. Large pursuers cannot walk through a corridor too narrow for them, though their reach can extend into its mouth. The model then estimates how much stopping/escape space a scout can reach without crossing likely interception areas. A distant pocket with a controlled entrance can score worse than an open position closer to an enemy.

The current scout implementation builds this extra map for one-square footprints on the existing single-level ground grid. It covers the scene up to 4,096 cells, otherwise a bounded local window. Travel estimates average alternating diagonal costs, use local grid expansion for reach, and conservatively ignore other creatures as blockers to pursuers. They are estimates of danger, not replacements for native movement, attack reach or reactions. Actual legal paths, costs, walls/surfaces, occupancy and terrain remain authoritative.

Map inputs include observed terrain, known or remembered enemy squares and previously observed reach/Speed. They do not query an Undetected enemy's live position, real defenses, HP or secret roll. An enemy outside the forecast or whose footprint cannot be modelled falls back to the previous conservative distance estimate. Geometry and result caches are keyed by relevant walls/regions, level, known occupants, known threat geometry and speed. They are evaluated during decisions and revalidation, not every frame.

Resolved movement destinations and each turn's starting square are retained for the current scene/combat, for at most two preceding rounds and twelve entries. Returning earns a capped score penalty, rather than becoming illegal. New encounters clear the context. Interrupted/skipped movement does not mark an unvisited planned destination. A useful attack, flank or escape can still outweigh the return cost.

The old full sight reward is reduced when it supplies no usable attack; it is absent for an empty-crossbow scout. Risky/hazardous negative approach scores no longer become zero merely because enemy distance decreases. Decision logs include resource readiness, the current grid summary, evaluated destinations and separate escape/history terms.

For the GM, select a scout that has acted since reload and run:

```js
await game.modules.get('foundry-creature-ai').api.tacticalMap();
```

This opens a read-only view of the last snapshot: green escape space, red restricted/interceptable squares, blocked grid connections, the scout and its known/remembered opponent positions. It is GM-only, not an automatic player overlay. Hover squares for arrival/space estimates. Later movement or wall edits are reflected when another decision is calculated; the viewer explicitly labels its snapshot round.

Regressions cover empty/deleted ammo, native Draw/approach/sword Strike, narrow passages, dead ends controlled at their entrances, terrain delays, wall-cache invalidation, unknown live-position invariance and cross-turn memory. The historical battle was inspected but not rerun. Reload every client with **Ctrl+F5**.

Version **0.21.0** adds two opportunities, calculated from the scene without mode checkboxes:

- **Group kiting:** a ranged scout values separation that makes melee pursuit costly while it retains a useful shot and other enabled, visible scouts can fire on that enemy. The small positioning preference saturates beyond the enemy's estimated one-Stride threat distance. It does not reward retreating indefinitely, moving into a blind pocket, or imaginary support from an ally behind a wall. Known enemy ranged attacks reduce the benefit of separation within their increment.
- **Swarm an isolated target:** when allies are already engaged, or at least two nearby enabled scouts could join before the target acts, a scout can draw its shortsword and enter melee. Other nearby known foes, unknown enemy locations, inability to flank, or lack of support prevent this isolation preference. Known reactive attacks damp the first-position reward; route hazards, possible reactions and weapon costs remain in the score. Existing actual flank opportunities can also justify drawing and moving without that isolation reward.

The first scout can take an anchor position that helps its allies surround the enemy. That is a bounded team-position preference, **not a grant of off-guard or predicted allied damage**. Precision from flanking is credited only when a real ally is currently able to flank from the appropriate opposite side. Native melee roll handling supplies off-guard/Sneak Attack. Crossbows do not receive flanking-based off-guard or precision; they can still use actual hiding or other applicable off-guard sources. See [Player Core flanking rules](https://2e.aonprd.com/Rules.aspx?ID=2375).

Remaining-turn forecasts now consider at most one future Step/Stride, using a shortlist of up to eight existing native legal routes. This permits a shot followed by a retreat, or drawing before moving and attacking, instead of evaluating only move-first sequences. Hand changes, MAP, bolts, movement cost and reactions retain their normal costs. A loaded scout may take its bow shot first, then draw and close for the next round. All plans are rebuilt after each action; allies are not ordered to move or teleported into the assumed support position.

The group sees living, observed same-alliance scouts in this combat. Ranged support requires a current firing lane and bolts; a potential melee helper must be close enough and able to act before the foe, unless already engaged. Calculations use known/remembered threat capabilities and geometry, not secret check results or enemy HP. Support and isolation are estimates: actual ally routes, changed positions and future player decisions are resolved when those turns occur. The existing native route, occupancy, reaction, terrain and interruption checks remain authoritative.

Decision logs retain group counts, support estimates and the selected kite/swarm goal; the utility table includes **Coordinated kite or swarm position**. Speech distinguishes falling back to keep shooting from drawing swords and circling a target. Tests cover complete native Draw/move/Strike execution, flanking precision restricted to melee, initiative, unseen/disabled/dead allies, reinforcements, unknown foes, support firing lanes, bounded kiting and dangerous routes. Version **0.20.0** applies lessons from the latest Ogre/Kobold battle:

- Compare the best remaining stationary attack, Reload, Hide and hand-change sequence after each candidate's first action. This accounts for action costs, MAP, finite bolts, lost hiding on revealing actions and a small credit for a loaded weapon next turn. A move's following shot is no longer compared with just one immediate shot. No forecast actions are committed; each actual result rebuilds the decision.
- Replace the summed cover reward with a bounded estimate of incoming pressure. Contact, one-Stride pursuit, two-Stride pursuit and distant enemies have different weights; known ranged threats also matter. Relative cover/concealment and the scout's estimated hiding reduce that pressure. A small cover improvement against several distant observers cannot accumulate into an overriding reward.
- Recognize threatened squares using observed enemy weapon reach, rather than just adjacency. Among legal moves, account for whether the enemy could close with a Stride. A minor cover gain cannot lure a firing scout into a new enemy's reach. Scamper still requires actual adjacency and a legal endpoint; it gets its native temporary AC bonus, not an unconditional utility reward. Useful retreats can compete with an attack even as the last action.
- Retain a small focus-fire preference (+0.35 per planned attack) for a target visibly hit by an allied scout during this or the previous round. Only witnesses who could see both attacker and target receive it. It expires, does not read HP, and does not outweigh a substantially better shot.

The GM's decision explanation includes **Estimated remaining sequence** and the expected attacks, readiness, defense, setup costs and reaction risks behind the score. Scoring remains an adaptation using the recovered Dawnsbury attack evaluator and chooser. The scout-specific complete-turn comparison uses the normal positive-action layer even for a last-action defensive move; other profiles retain their existing layer behavior. These heuristics are explicit policy changes, not ongoing machine learning or another extracted Dawnsbury decision tree.

Reach, land Speed and the presence/range of ranged attacks are refreshed only for observed opponents and remembered for this combat/scene. Unobserved weapon changes do not update that knowledge. Unknown threats use conservative generic estimates. Position forecasts use known or remembered squares. Actual defenses, current enemy HP and secret Hide/Sneak outcomes are excluded; success probabilities still come from the scout's own skill, known cover and the existing encounter benchmark. The existing first-use stealth showcase and frontline preference remain, with showcase limited by a new useful benefit.

## Use

1. Reload all clients and set up [cover/concealment regions](environment-zones.md). A smoke rectangle is a good first test; an empty open map should not let this scout Hide.
2. Put its unlinked token in combat with opposing-alliance characters. Select the scout, press **Alt+W**, check **Crossbow currently loaded**, and save/run. Loaded is a persistent state, not an infinite ammunition supply. Correct that checkbox after firing/reloading manually.
3. Try multiple characters with different Perception DCs. The GM's action report shows the separate detection results.
4. Use the **eye icon — Detection controls** to inspect/correct each observer's state after manual actions or adjudication. Since **0.18.0**, taught NPCs [Seek automatically](npc-perception.md) during their turns. Point Out and player Seek remain manual.

## Supported behavior

- **Hide** costs one action and rolls the native secret Stealth check automatically. The same d20 is compared separately against each observer's Perception DC, with relative cover bonuses. A scout can be Hidden to Aisa and Observed by Fighter simultaneously. Standard/greater cover or concealment permits Hide; complete sight-blocking obstacles also permit hiding. Lesser cover alone does not.
- **Sneak** costs one action, uses a legal native path no longer than half Speed, and checks only observers the scout was already Hidden/Undetected from. Success becomes Undetected; failure stays Hidden; critical failure becomes Observed where sight permits. The secret roll resolves before native movement so a failed Sneak never receives temporary immunity to Reactive Strike. Native movement, reactions and damage complete before AI resumes. A Sneak cover bonus is granted when one protected-area region continuously contains the whole path; ambiguous continuous cover across multiple regions/intervening obstacles receives no automatic bonus and can be adjudicated manually.
- **Knowledge** keeps the old known square for an observer who loses the scout to successful Sneak. Other observers can continue tracking normal/failed movement. The module does not set global actor Hidden or GM-hide the token.
- **Player display** suppresses an Undetected scout through Foundry's native token visibility getter for users whose owned observers cannot locate it. GM view remains available. A user owning multiple characters sees it if at least one can locate it. This is client presentation, not a security boundary against inspecting scene data. Sneak/Undetected Step suppresses the native movement ruler to avoid displaying the new path.
- **Strike** uses native crossbow/shortsword attack and damage rolls. A target the scout is Hidden/Undetected from is off-guard to that attack; the existing native Sneak Attack rule receives the off-guard roll option. The scout reveals after its Strike resolves, including on a miss or failed targeting flat check. Existing prone/flanking/off-guard conditions also enable precision normally.
- **Reload** costs one Interact, requires bolts and a ready crossbow, reveals before provoking manipulate reactions, and does not refill inventory. Firing consumes one bolt and unloads the bow, including on a failed concealment check. A new token starts loaded by default; the GM controls its initial state.
- **Equipment**: drawing the shortsword costs one Interact after releasing a crossbow hand for free. The bow remains held in one hand and cannot fire. Stowing the sword and restoring a two-handed bow grip cost separate Interacts. Empty/deleted bolt stacks do not prevent melee behavior or fabricate new ammunition.
- **Scamper** costs one action, requires an adjacent known enemy, allows Speed +5 feet, and plans an endpoint not adjacent to any known enemy. It can still provoke; native reaction attacks receive its +2 circumstance AC bonus during that move. The temporary marker clears even if movement is interrupted.
- Stand, Escape, normal Step/Stride/Crawl, action/MAP reservations, reaction waits, native damage and Shield Block, death/recovery and optional automatic advancement use the shared framework.

## Decision policy and limits

The profile uses the recovered Dawnsbury attack evaluator, including concealment chances, and ranks scout-specific remaining-turn plans through its adapted positive-action layer. Foundry-specific candidates compare hiding for precision/defense, reloading, useful cover/firing positions, half-Speed sneaking and escaping melee. Entertainment preferences include a bounded first Hide showcase and first front-line engagement. Hide is normally limited to one attempt per turn by policy. Sneak can repeat while actions remain; each destination still needs a useful firing position, protection, approach, escape, regained sight, or defensive Undetected benefit. An already-undetected scout does not shuffle between equivalent squares solely to Sneak again.

The scout stores its own per-observer belief separately in `stealthBelief`. Estimated probabilities use its prepared Stealth modifier, known cover and the existing encounter-level defense benchmark as an approximate Perception DC. They never read the secret d20, check total, degree or an opponent's real Perception DC. The first Hide after revealing establishes an estimated probability of being unseen; repeating Hide without feedback does not inflate that confidence. Sneak combines the prior with estimated success/critical-failure chances. Precision attack values weight both the ordinary and unseen cases instead of assuming success. This probability model is a new Foundry adaptation, not an extracted Dawnsbury stealth rule.

Step and Sneak retain expected hidden-attack credit only against observers from whom the endpoint remains protected. Sneak discounts that credit for its estimated failure risk. Normal movement and Reload clear the belief, with an explicit cost for losing expected protection. A secretly failed Hide can still be followed by an attempted Sneak: it rolls, moves at half Speed and spends an action while GM execution keeps the scout observed by observers who already saw it. This prevents free cancellation from revealing the secret result. Without an earlier hiding estimate or a protected half-Speed endpoint, ordinary movement remains available. Repeated Sneak alone cannot improve an already-high estimate of being undetected.

The GM's separate `stealth` record remains authoritative for actual visibility, known-square memory, reactions, native off-guard and precision damage. A creature's perception of an opponent remains valid sensory information; opponents do not receive the subject's private roll or belief. Saved detection corrections do not automatically tell the subject whether its secret roll succeeded. Old records without a belief start with no assumed hiding benefit until the scout attempts Hide again.

When multiple actions remain, the scout readies its crossbow or melee weapon before an offensive Hide setup, so it does not immediately throw away a successful Hide by reloading or drawing. It can still Hide defensively as its last action with an unloaded weapon. Reload lookahead does not assume hidden precision damage survives reloading.

The inspected DD `AI.HideSelf()` is **not** a general stealth decision tree: it gives a priority override to a particular Demon Horizonwalker setup and otherwise returns an exclusion sentinel. This profile does not copy that creature-specific override to kobolds. Its stealth action selection and observer-relative Foundry execution are explicit new adaptations; the shared chooser and attack-concealment evaluator are recovered DD behavior.

This profile assumes ordinary visual observation. Darkness/light levels, special senses, undetected square guessing, automatic Point Out, traps and drawing/stowing arbitrary equipment still need GM handling. Native wall/Toolbelt cover on attack rolls is preserved, but AI hiding behind low walls needs an explicit region. Continuous-cover bonuses and per-observer check results are summarized in GM reports rather than represented by global actor effects. Other taught NPCs can Seek a hidden scout automatically; a located target can be attacked with the appropriate targeting check. Invisibility caps ordinary visual discovery at Hidden. Player Hide/Sneak interception is implemented separately in [player stealth](player-stealth.md).

Automated tests cover the exported mechanics, mixed observer results, hidden precision attacks, failed Sneak/reactions, reload/ammunition/hand costs, movement limits, player visibility, known-square memory, complete turns and prior profiles. A running-world multiplayer smoke test is still needed.
