## Decisions

The recovered Dawnsbury comparator remains the common ranking basis. The spell valuations are explicit Foundry adaptations, rather than a claim of decompiled caster-policy parity. The planner uses the existing encounter AC benchmark to estimate attack/save chances; it does not read actual opposing saves/AC or secret check results for scoring.

Limited spells carry a resource cost, scaled from 35% to 100% of its normal value using the caster's own remaining HP fraction. Area value counts expected enemy benefit and penalizes known allies, the caster and bystanders. Unknown/undetected creatures are not used to aim or choose areas, but actual area execution can still affect them if they are physically inside. Geometry uses the same remembered squares as the other profiles. Tangle Vine rejects already-tangled or immobilized targets. Grease separates falling immediately from later movement restrictions: an already-prone creature can still be hindered by the surface, and a prone ally carries a real movement cost. Flying and already-greased creatures receive no new ground-control benefit. Friendly fire is penalized, rather than universally forbidden.

The planner compares **cast now**, **move then cast**, **cast then move**, and affordable Torch combinations. Forecasts track action costs, spell slots, spell-showcase history and attack MAP. A known safe Step can remove casting reaction exposure. Each forecast executes only its first action; the runner then waits for reactions/results and recomputes everything. It never assumes an attack hit or a secret check succeeded. A new Grease cast does not forecast movement over pre-cast paths, since its terrain changes those paths and Balance requirements.

Retreat value estimates plausible melee pressure using visible weapon roles/reach and a benchmark 25-foot Speed. It counts the strongest pursuer plus a quarter of other pursuers' pressure, capped at 6; relief below 1.5 earns no retreat bonus. This is a preference, not predicted damage or an obstacle-aware enemy-turn simulation. Ranged weapon users generate less closing pressure and receive less movement-denial value than approaching melee enemies. Roles are learned only while observed and remembered within the current combat/scene. Hidden equipment changes, opposing defenses, ammunition counts and secret roll results do not supply role updates. Prototype reset clears the new memory as well.

Signature spell preferences remain capped at +2 once per spell per encounter, and do not turn a net harmful or ineffective area into a candidate. Short buff openings and support spells still require future taught profiles; the Pyro has no such opening or supported buff.

Two-action casts and attack MAP are reserved before asynchronous rolls or mutations. Native slot consumption commits the spell before a reaction can disrupt it; a disrupted spell keeps its spent actions/slot and produces no spell effect. Grabbed and Stupefied casting flat checks are handled as well. Automatic checks bypass roll dialogs and wait for completion. Automatic damage uses the shared reaction barrier and normal PF2e damage application; rolls-only mode leaves damage buttons available and pauses the AI.

All reachable destinations receive targeted-spell estimates. Cone and Grease enumeration is bounded to twelve promising destinations when enough actions remain to move and cast. The caster prefers room to cast, avoids entering immediate melee solely for stronger melee cantrip dice, and avoids a useless final approach when it already has a valid next-turn spell position. With one ordinary action left and no useful supported action, it can deliberately finish and auto-advance when enabled. This is distinct from a blocked or unresolved action, whose remaining budget is preserved for correction.

## Defensive setup

When all visible, reachable-by-sight opponents are apparent melee users beyond its 30-foot engagement range, the Pyro suppresses movement whose only benefit is closing to cast. Nearby cover and concealment grant a bounded positional benefit. A move into protection can include an affordable Hide follow-up. Against ranged opponents, or while investigating a lost enemy, ordinary engagement and pursuit remain available. An immediate useful spell still competes with setup over the same remaining action budget.

Hide can be useful after casting or while waiting for enemies. Its belief is stored separately from the actual secret result: failure does not tell the caster to immediately Hide again. It can attempt Hide once per turn, and does not repeat it against observers for whom its existing belief provides the same benefit. Casting, ordinary Stride and other revealing actions clear that belief; Step retains it only while the hiding geometry remains valid. Casting reveals before casting reactions are considered; a Torch Strike reveals after the attempt and can apply actual hidden/off-guard targeting. These timings follow [Hide](https://2e.aonprd.com/Actions.aspx?ID=2404). The shared stealth component can now offer Sneak for a defensive move between protected positions; it does not award an offensive bonus merely for becoming Undetected.

Grease now considers empty 2×2 areas on a direct approach by an **observed melee enemy 30–60 feet away**. Each square must be within casting range and have a clear spell path. Native movement constraints check the approaching foe's footprint along the predicted lane. Future control value is discounted by distance, capped across foes, and charged against the normal slot and bystander costs. This is a prediction of a clear lane, not a full enemy path planner: enemies remain free to detour or attack somebody else. A caster with an active owned Grease zone does not receive another empty-ground preparation reward. Ordinary occupied-target Grease remains available.

Once setup offers no further useful action, the caster can finish a waiting turn with unused actions and auto-advance if enabled. The report explains that it is holding its casting position. Two consecutive quiet setup turns permit ordinary cautious engagement again, preventing an indefinite standoff. Prototype reset clears both this patience history and Hide-attempt state.

## Persistent Grease

The visible Grease Region has native Modify Movement Cost and module-owned behavior attachments. The native terrain engine handles overlapping difficult terrain; the module does not multiply that cost again. Step and Crawl ignore this Grease difficulty, but ordinary difficult terrain from other sources still applies. The ruler normally assumes a successful Balance check. A critical Balance success ignores the Grease difficulty for that resolved movement.

One drag represents one move action. Ground movement crossing Grease is routed through the GM movement workflow even when ordinary reactive-movement prompting is disabled. Step/Crawl do not make Grease checks. For other movement, the GM rolls the higher prepared Reflex or Acrobatics statistic once for the move action. Success treats Grease as difficult terrain; critical success permits normal Speed. Failure chooses the remain-stationary outcome and spends the action. Critical failure replays the path only as far as the first Grease square, then applies Prone and ends an automated creature's turn. If movement starts on Grease, the fall happens in place. Normal PC action counts and manual turn advancement remain the player's/GM's responsibility. Checks are resolved before the move replay; the path is shortened if necessary to fit its legal Speed budget.

If native movement stops a falling creature at a Region boundary, a zero-cost native displacement settles it on a legal grid placement on Grease, rather than before the surface. It uses native token snapping and coverage for the creature's footprint. The correction respects walls, occupied spaces, elevation and encounter cancellation, and does not trigger another move action, hazard step or reaction. Reports retain the travelled path cost even when Foundry's latest movement is that zero-cost correction. An external Prone source does not itself forfeit the creature's remaining actions; only the known critical Balance failure does. Other native stopped or constrained movement keeps its action spent and rebuilds the next choice from the actual position. Explicit Stop, game pause and unresolved reactions still pause automation.

While a grounded creature occupies Grease, a module-owned effect grants off-guard. It is removed on exit or expiry without deleting other off-guard sources. Flying/burrowing/swimming movement is excluded. This first profile operates on one scene level/elevation; vertical area aiming is not supported.

Grease expires at the caster's turn ten rounds later in combat, or after 60 seconds of world time outside that combat. Deleting its combat removes its zones. The prototype reset macro removes Grease and owned Tangle Vine effects cast by reset tokens, clears casting-showcase history and recalculates zone indicators. Other actors' spells and unrelated Regions are preserved.
